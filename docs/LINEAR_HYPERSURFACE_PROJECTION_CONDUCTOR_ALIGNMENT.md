# Actual finite-dimensional hypersurface projection equations and homogeneous conductor fibres

Source: companionT1.7, arithmetic_appendix.tex:480, and its chosen-source and ordinary surface-cover branches. Primary comparison: Salberger, Counting rational points on projective varieties (2023), §7, https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.12508; these algebraic lemmas do not assert the determinant-method cover. Original manuscript hashes remain unchanged.

LinearHypersurfaceImage extends the actual plane-image construction to an arbitrary finite family g of m degree-one parameters and one actual degree-one coordinate y. Its coordinate algebra is the literal polynomial quotient by the actual evaluation kernel in m+1 variables, rather than a supplied abstract image. The quotient injects into the original algebra, its ideal is homogeneous and prime for an original domain, and the genuine quotient grading and finite-dimensional pieces are constructed. The parameter map and the map to the original algebra are finite whenever the original parameter evaluation is finite; parameter injectivity follows from the original parameter injectivity.

LinearHypersurfaceMinimalEquation proves that the actual image kernel is generated by the actual ring minimal polynomial of y, transported through the literal Fin-successor polynomial equivalence. Its equation is nonzero, monic in the distinguished coordinate, irreducible and homogeneous. No plane-curve Hilbert computation or curve-only dimension hypothesis is used. The actual ring minimal-polynomial degree is proved equal to the degree over the fraction field of the polynomial parameter ring. When y is primitive for the original function field, the resulting homogeneous hypersurface equation has total degree exactly the original field-extension degree. Finiteness and injectivity internally supply integrality and torsion freedom in the assembled equation theorem.

LinearHypersurfaceImageFiber proves for this genuine image that the derivative of its ring minimal polynomial evaluates to the actual distinguished-coordinate partial derivative, is homogeneous of degree d-1, and is nonzero in the original domain in the separable case. The trace-dual conductor theorem already established for curves applies over the parameter polynomial ring of any dimension: multiplication by this actual derivative takes every original coordinate into the actual image. Hence original point evaluations agreeing on the actual image are equal whenever this derivative does not vanish. For three parameters this supplies the exact algebraic hypersurface/conductor framework needed for a surface projection into projective three-space.

These are proved algebraic projection ingredients, not a surface-cover theorem. The actual original-source three-parameter normalization, uniform height specialization, geometric-integrality transport, pullback decomposition of covering curves and the determinant-method covering bound still require their own proofs.
