# One fixed Gaussian parameter makes every actual bad logarithmic window uniformly small

Source: internal preparatory analytic route for companion T19.17. This is not a completed additional manuscript result.

One theorem proves that for every epsilon>0, one actual D>0 and one integer cutoff precede all N, every smoothness cutoff Y, every j in [N,2N) whose actual logarithmic window contains at most one smooth integer, and every real center in its middle unit cell. The literal short Gaussian average is at most epsilon. The proof chooses D from the proved positive tail rate, controls the actual singleton term by a logarithmic limit, and retains the exact floor window and population. No bad-window Gaussian bound is assumed.

The actual off-central estimate, the ordinary smooth long-interval mean comparison and the uniform Gaussian mean-square comparison are proved. Localization of the Gaussian averages to the literal logarithmic windows remains unproved, so T19.17 stays conditional. The stronger uniform sharp short-average theorem for all real completely multiplicative functions is not established. T19.17 conditional; original T1.7 unconditional.
