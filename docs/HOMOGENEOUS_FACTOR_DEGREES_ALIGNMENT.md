# Homogeneity of actual factors from exact lowest and highest total degrees

Source: companion T1.4 non-geometric biform branch; the actual projective identification remaining after P1-MATH-0326. Original manuscript hashes remain preserved.

A needed algebraic step for the actual projective identification is now proved: every factor of a nonzero homogeneous multivariate polynomial over an integral domain is homogeneous, with degree equal to its own actual total degree. No homogeneous-factor decomposition is assumed.

The proof compares the highest and lowest actual total degrees. Embedding a multivariate polynomial into formal power series gives its lowest-degree order. For a nonzero polynomial, this order is at most its total degree; for a homogeneous polynomial it equals the homogeneous degree. Conversely, equality of the series order and the polynomial total degree forces every nonzero coefficient to have that degree, proving homogeneity.

In an integral domain, actual polynomial total degrees add under a nonzero product, and formal-series orders add exactly. If the product is homogeneous, the two sums coincide. The individual lower-degree orders are bounded by the individual upper degrees, so each pair is equal. This proves both factors homogeneous, using only the checked degree/order identities and finite coefficient extensionality. The preparatory order and homogeneity criteria work already over commutative semirings; the no-zero-divisor domain assumption is used at the product step.
