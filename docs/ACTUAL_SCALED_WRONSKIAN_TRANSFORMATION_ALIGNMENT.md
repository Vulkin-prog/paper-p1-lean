# Exact actual Wronskian transformation under smooth differential scaling

Sources rechecked: main13.4, main_sections/pair_rigidity.tex:211-226; primary BCN arXiv:2302.04209v2 Sections3-4 and the change from the polynomial tangent field to normalized graph fields, https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04209v2. This is a proved analytic transformation bridge, not a global finite-cover theorem.

ScaledDerivativeExpansion constructs the exact universal expansion coefficients for an actual scaled differential chain F_k_prime=a F_(k+1). The coefficient recurrence differentiates the previous row and adds the shifted row multiplied by a. Coefficients above the diagonal are proved identically zero as actual functions, and the exact diagonal is a(t)^n. On any open domain where a is C-infinity, every coefficient is C-infinity. A finite successor-sum identity and the actual product/sum derivative rules then prove the full n-th ordinary derivative expansion of F_0. Only the actual first-derivative chain equations are supplied; no iterated derivative identity is an input.

ScaledWronskianTransformation turns these coefficients into an actual lower triangular matrix. Its determinant is exactly a(t)^(sum_{i:Fin n} i), hence is nonzero at every parameter with a(t) nonzero. The actual ordinary derivative jet matrix of a finite family is proved equal to this transformation matrix times the actual differential-chain evaluation matrix. The classical Wronskian therefore equals the exact scale power times the chain determinant. The nonzero-Wronskian consequence is internally proved for every family size, including the empty-matrix convention.

This is the general analytic bridge needed when polynomial tangent trajectories are normalized or affinely reparametrized. It verifies that a nonzero differential scale changes Wronskians by a nonzero factor, rather than assuming that ordinary derivatives are unchanged by reparametrization. The theorem uses actual derivative equations, actual finite sums and actual determinants. No global trajectory, diffeomorphism or analytic continuation premise is hidden in the statement.
