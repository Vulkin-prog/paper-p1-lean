# Randomized orientation: full-private law and square-anchor scope test

Sources: companion T19.16 and T19.17, limits_appendix.tex:1214 and1313, including the clean-pair constructions in their proofs. All original source hashes are preserved in the numbered ledger.

Companion T19.17 gains an exact scope test for the randomized
conditional-law paragraph and a proof of that law under the essential
fully private hypothesis. This does not refute the printed numerical
obstruction. Its randomized cost aggregation and ordinary source census
remain open, so the numbered result stays in progress.

The randomized paragraph follows a restriction to sources whose entire
maximal support has pivots above Y0. That restriction must be retained
when asserting that fairness of g(U,xi) gives the conditional source law.
Extending the assertion to the WHOLE original one-small-pivot retained
population is false. The distinction is now checked in the actual model.

OddPivotSpecialValues proves the exact actual pivots of every square
and prime. RandomOrientationSquareAnchor uses the concrete word (4,5),
start 5, maximal width 2, cutoff Y=4, dyadic parameter N=3 and prime
cylinder M=8. Its square anchor has pivot 1; its only other pivot is
the private prime 5>4. The word is proved to belong to the original
at-most-one-small-pivot retained population and its unsigned event has
actual probability 1/2.

Take independent fair auxiliary xi and g(U,xi)=xi, which is fair for
a uniform raw source word, and even for each fixed original sample.
The actual map changes only private prime 5 and sets the nonanchor
source value to its prescribed template plus xi. The square 4 keeps
raw bit zero under EVERY sample. Consequently the forced unsigned
start event holds exactly when xi=1, with probability 1/2 instead of
one. The theorem not_full_conditional_law explicitly refutes the full
conditional-observable identity for this map, using the event itself
as an observable. This is a local conditional-law scope counterexample,
not an asymptotic counterexample to the numerical lower bound.

FairBinaryMixture handles an ARBITRARY finite auxiliary probability,
not merely uniform auxiliary bits. A fair F2-valued function of the
original uniform source word and independent auxiliary randomness gives
the exact half-and-half mixture of its two outputs for every real
observable. No independence of g from the source is required.

RandomizedPrivatePalmForcing requires every ORIGINAL raw source
coordinate to have its actual private prime basis vector. It changes
only those prime coordinates and hits the target unsigned orbit for
every sample and auxiliary draw. For every fixed background chart,
uniform source coordinates and the fair orientation give exactly the
equal mixture of the two specified signed forcings. Averaging the
actual prime-cylinder chart then proves the complete unsigned
conditional law for EVERY real observable of the full sample. Its
exact event rate is 2^(-(m-1)) for a nonempty source word of length m.
The weighted conditional identity and preservation of every nonselected
prime are also proved. The fairness input is solely the prescribed
orientation condition; no Palm law or probability value is a premise.

Thus the random-orientation paragraph is valid on the fully private
good sources used in the lower count. On other retained sources one
must keep the unforced anchor compatible with the imposed orientation,
for example by the already proved anchor-preserving map. Merely
requiring an unconstrained fair sign is insufficient there. The
original deterministic-anchor theorem and its conditional obstruction
are unchanged. A global hybrid randomized field and the half-factor
cost aggregation still need their own formalization.
