\section*{How the estimates fit together}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How the estimates fit together}
The model, exact marginals, effective relation spaces and centered
coefficients are defined in Sections~\ref{p1:intro:model}
and~\ref{p13:exact:section} of the article. References without a
T prefix refer to that article. Both documents are needed for the
complete proof.

\paragraph{Pair coefficients and independent fixed moments.}
Sections~\ref{p1:arith:appendix}--\ref{p1:arith:supplement}
give the arithmetic pair estimates. The elementary residual power
$6/5$ suffices for the first pair profile; its later quasi-linear
strengthening is not used to prove itself. Section~\ref{p1:fixed:section}
establishes the fixed moments independently of the approximation of
the law. The good-pair compression estimate remains necessary in this
moment proof.

\paragraph{The gain below the quadratic scale.}
Short-selection counts, conditional half-counts, confinement and
pair rigidity culminate in the triple analysis of
Section~\ref{p13:third:section}. Its positive and signed estimates
give $A_3\ll N^{-41/30+\varepsilon}$. The all-target Palm estimate
is a consequence of the positive connected-triple mass and the
absolute marginal bias. It is not used as a premise for that mass.

\paragraph{Summing the entire expansion.}
The elementary certificates of Section~\ref{p14:cert:section}
are used in Section~\ref{p2:technical-transfer}. The latter proves
separate finite-order estimates and a uniform tail beyond order
$256$. Its homogeneous primitive estimate rests on the geometric
dense-host construction and the half-count, not on a law approximation.
The article applies these estimates to its finite stopped expansion.

\paragraph{Two useful consequences.}
The uniform support estimate in Section~\ref{p1:uniform:appendix}
combines finitely many half-power layers with one independent tail.
Its threshold and constant are uniform in the lower support size.
The final finite-difference example explains why estimates at all
fixed moment orders alone cannot supply a polynomial distance rate.

In every asymptotic assertion, the critical-band constant, accuracy
parameters, fixed source counts and fixed channel heights precede
the scale threshold. Growing degrees and cardinalities occur only
in estimates that state their uniform range explicitly.
