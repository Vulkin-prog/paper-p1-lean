\paragraph{The asymmetric alignment radius.}
In Lemma~\ref{p1:arith:asymmetric}, the exclusion radius now follows the
old enclosing interval: radius $DB$ is used for an old subset of
$X+[-DB,DB]$.  The exact proved domain has
$N\leq X<N+QN$ and $z\in I_N$, with fixed $Q\geq1$;
the choice $Q=1$ gives both centres in the original dyadic block.
The cutoff interval is explicitly
$(2D+1)B\leq H\leq D_{\rm cut}B$.
The full weight $2^{\delta^*}-1$ and exponent $31/24+\varepsilon$
are retained.  Compressed-source applications use the same enlarged
radius in their complementary aligned sector.

The radius qualification is necessary.  In the version~1.6 radius-one
convention, a checked family with $L=3k$, $B=3k+1$,
$X=2t$, $z=3t+3B$, and $U_X=[X,X+3B]\cap\Z$ has
$\delta^*\geq k-1$ above every cutoff $H\geq3$.
For $t>5B^4$ it is nonaligned at radius one but aligned at radius two.
A dyadic subfamily has weighted mass at least a fixed multiple of
$N^{4/3}$, contradicting the claimed radius-one
$N^{31/24+\varepsilon}$ bound for sufficiently small fixed
$\varepsilon>0$.
The corrected full estimates are checked by
\texttt{SharpAsymmetricProfile.uniform\_profile} and
\texttt{SharpExpandedAsymmetricProfile.uniform\_profile}.

\paragraph{The exact quadratic exponent.}
Lemma~\ref{p13:short:quadratic} uses the capped exponent
\[
 c_2(\beta,\delta)=
 \min\{1,\beta-\delta,\max(\beta/2-\delta,1-\beta/2)\}.
\]
The exact affine inequality in its statement is now valid for every
$\beta,\delta$.  The extra entry comes from the trivial count $O(N)$;
the two arithmetic estimates and their uncapped minimum remain valid.
For arbitrary real parameters the uncapped exact affine comparison holds if
$\beta-2\delta\leq2$.  When $\delta\geq0$ this condition is also
necessary; this includes $\delta=\log e/\log N$ for $e>0$ and $N\geq2$.

This distinction occurs on admissible data at arbitrarily large sizes:
for squarefree consecutive $N,N+1$, take $h=e=1$, $n=N$, and
$\beta=\log(N(N+1))/\log N>2$.  Then $\delta=0$ and the uncapped
minimum is $\beta/2>1/2+\beta/4$.
The actual capped and affine counting conclusions are checked by
\texttt{PrescribedQuadraticKernelTheorem.actual\_exponent\_counts};
the exact capped comparison is
\texttt{QuadraticKernelExponents.capped\_le\_affine}.

\paragraph{The sufficient ordinary smooth-window census.}
For fixed $a>0$ put $Y=\lfloor N^a\rfloor$ and
$h_N=\lfloor\log N/(4\log2)\rfloor$.
The precise internally proved statement is
\[
 \#\left\{j\in I_N:
   \#\{n\in[j,j+h_N)\cap\Z:P^+(n)\leq Y\}\leq1\right\}
   =o_a(N).
\]
Here $P^+(1)=1$.  Equivalently, for every $\tau>0$, one cutoff
precedes all $N$ for which the displayed exceptional cardinality
is at most $\tau N$.  The canonical declarations are:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
SmoothLogarithmicWindowDensity.uniform\_bound\\
UnconditionalOrdinarySourceCensus.ordinary\_census.
\end{quote}
Only this sufficient statement is needed for
Proposition~\ref{p1:limits:all-anchors}.

We describe the proof and its exact analytic scope.
Write $\chi_Y$ for the ordinary smooth indicator and
$g_Y(d)=\mu(d)\ind_{\gcd(d,\prod_{p\leq Y}p)=1}$.
The exact identity $\chi_Y=1*g_Y$ gives, for
$S_Y(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_Y(n)$,
\[
 S_Y(x)=\sum_{d\leq x}g_Y(d)\lfloor x/d\rfloor .
\]
For a common cutoff $K\geq x,z>0$,
\[
 \left|\frac{S_Y(x)}x-\frac{S_Y(z)}z\right|
 \leq R_Y(K)\left(\frac1x+\frac1z\right),
 \quad
 R_Y(K)=\#\{d\leq K:\gcd(d,\prod_{p\leq Y}p)=1\}.
\]
The actual primorial Selberg sieve with exponent
$\min(a,1/8)$ gives $R_Y(4N)\ll_a N/\log N$.
Together with the positive dyadic ordinary smooth density, this proves
uniform positivity of averages on intervals of length
$H_N=\lceil N/\sqrt{\log N}\rceil$.
The explicit difference from the dyadic mean is
$O_a((\log N)^{-1/2})$.

For $b>0$ let
\[
 \gamma_b(v)=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi b}}e^{-v^2/(4b)},\qquad
 G_b(u)=\sum_{N\leq n<4N}\frac{\chi_Y(n)}n
                  \gamma_b(u+\log n),
\]
and put
$F(t)=\sum_{N\leq n<4N}\chi_Y(n)n^{-1-it}$.
The exact finite Gaussian identity is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|G_b-G_c|^2\,du
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|F(t)|^2
                 (e^{-bt^2}-e^{-ct^2})^2\,dt .
\]
All integrability and finite-sum interchanges are included in the
formal proof.
Choose $b=(\log N/(DN))^2$ for fixed $D>0$, and
$c=(H_N/N)^2$.
The actual off-central Dirichlet estimate, applied to the two dyadic
blocks, makes the frequency integral small away from a fixed central
band.  On that band the difference of the Gaussian weights tends to
zero.  The remaining tail is bounded by the unconditional Gaussian
mean-value estimate
$\sqrt{\pi/b}/N+4\pi$ and its exponential tail factor.
Consequently $\int|G_b-G_c|^2=o_D(1)$, uniformly in the real
completely multiplicative coefficients allowed in that comparison.
The canonical analytic conclusion is
\texttt{ActualGaussianMeanComparison.uniform\_bound}.

The long smooth averages give $G_c(-\log X)\geq\kappa_a>0$
for $N\leq X$ and $X+1\leq2N$.
If a window $[j,j+h_N)$ has at most one smooth integer, then for
$j+h_N/2\leq X\leq j+h_N/2+1$ its short Gaussian average is at most
$O_D(1/\log N)+O(e^{-c_0D^2})$ for a fixed $c_0>0$.
The first term bounds the possible single interior integer; the
second bounds all exterior integers using their actual logarithmic
distance.  First choose $D$ so this is at most $\kappa_a/2$, then
choose the common integer cutoff.

The middle unit cells of distinct integer windows have disjoint
negative-logarithmic images, each of measure at least $1/(4N)$.
Thus the number of interior bad windows is at most
$16N\int|G_b-G_c|^2/\kappa_a^2=o_a(N)$.
The omitted endpoint windows number at most $h_N=o(N)$.
This proves the sufficient census.
The exact original deterministic coefficient and the randomized
factor one half are then supplied by
\texttt{UnconditionalPrintedAnchorObstruction.companion\_T19\_17}
and
\texttt{UnconditionalPrintedAnchorObstruction.companion\_T19\_17\_randomized}.

\paragraph{A stronger finite prime-cylinder bound.}
The construction in Proposition~\ref{p1:limits:cylinder} also gives a
stronger finite estimate.  Let $q\leq N$ be prime, put $L=q-1$ and
$M=2q\lfloor N/q\rfloor\leq2N$, and prescribe $f(q)=-1$ and all other prime signs up to
$M$ to be positive.  Retain the starts $x=qu+1$ with
\[
 \lfloor N/q\rfloor+1\leq u<2\lfloor N/q\rfloor,
 \qquad q\nmid u.
\]
Each has a negative predecessor and at least $q-1$ positive values,
all at integers at most $M$.  The signs above $M$ remain free, so this
cylinder has probability $2^{-\pi(M)}$.
The extra condition $q\nmid(u+1)$ would force a negative right endpoint;
it is unnecessary for a start of length at least $q-1$.
Counting the single excluded residue class, including the floor and
endpoint errors, gives at least $N/q-N/q^2-3$ retained starts.
Their restricted count is bounded by the full positive count;
integrability and monotonicity give, for every real $t\geq0$,
\[
 \E e^{tZ_{N,q-1}}\ \geq\ \E e^{tZ^+_{N,q-1}}
 \ \geq\
 \exp\!\left\{t\left(\frac Nq-\frac N{q^2}-3\right)
                    -\pi(M)\log2\right\},
\]
where $Z^+$ counts positive starts only.  This improves the earlier
finite lower count $N/q-2N/q^2-4$; the printed odd-prime scale,
$t>2$ domain and $(t-2)\log2$ limiting coefficient are preserved.
The canonical checked bounds are
\texttt{SupportedPrimeCylinder.positive\_exponential\_lower} and
\texttt{SupportedPrimeCylinder.total\_exponential\_lower}.
At the original scale $q=7$, $N=64$, the new cutoff $126$ charges
30 primes, whereas the earlier sufficient cutoff $134$ charges 32.
With the same retained-count contribution, the finite exponential lower
bound therefore gains a factor four; this exact example is also checked.

\paragraph{Exact valuations and finite layers.}
Within the displayed multiplier interval, a positive start occurs
precisely when $\nu_q(u)$ is even: its predecessor has valuation
$1+\nu_q(u)$, and its following $q-1$ values are positive.
The coprime multipliers form the layer $\nu_q(u)=0$.
For example, $N=18$, $q=3$ also admits $u=9$, adding the start $28$.
All these additional starts use the same supported cylinder.

For every integer depth $k\geq0$, retaining the disjoint layers
$\nu_q(u)=2j$, $0\leq j<k$, gives the explicit lower count
\[
 B_k(N,q)=\frac{N}{q+1}\bigl(1-q^{-2k}\bigr)-3k.
\]
Exact counts of multiples in translated intervals give the endpoint
error $3k$; the geometric sum is evaluated exactly.  For every $t\geq0$,
both full exponential moments are therefore at least
$\exp\{tB_k(N,q)-\pi(M)\log2\}$.
The choice $k=1$ recovers $N/q-N/q^2-3$.
At the original critical scale $q=17$, $N=2^{16}$, the budget for
$k=2$ is strictly greater, giving a strictly stronger exponential
lower bound for every $t>0$ at the same cost.
Larger depth need not improve this explicit estimate because its
endpoint error grows.  Here $k$ is the number of retained valuation layers.
The original $t>2$ domain and limiting coefficient remain unchanged.
The canonical declarations are
\texttt{ValuationPrimeCylinder.positive\_start\_iff\_even\_factorization}
and \texttt{ValuationLayerCounts.finite\_geometric\_lower}, with both
actual moment bounds in the latter module.

\paragraph{Effective density of the retained starts.}
Let $C(N,q)$ denote the complete retained start count above, and put
$\kappa(N,q)=\lceil\tfrac12\lceil\log_q N\rceil\rceil$.
Every retained multiplier satisfies $0<u<N\leq q^{2\kappa(N,q)}$.
Its even valuation is therefore $2j$ for exactly one
$0\leq j<\kappa(N,q)$, so these finitely many disjoint layers cover
the complete retained set.  Their exact multiple counts give
\[
 \frac{N-1}{q+1}-3\kappa(N,q)\leq C(N,q)
 \leq\frac{N}{q+1}+2\kappa(N,q).
\]
Both full exponential moments consequently have the lower bound
$\exp\{t[(N-1)/(q+1)-3\kappa(N,q)]-\pi(M)\log2\}$ for $t\geq0$.
This depth gives a complete cover; it need not maximize $B_k$.
For each fixed prime $q$, the ratio $C(N,q)/N$ tends to $1/(q+1)$.
Moreover, simultaneously for all primes $q\leq N$,
\[
 \left|\frac{C(N,q)}N-\frac1{q+1}\right|
 \leq\frac{3\kappa(N,2)+1}{N}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The approximation threshold depends only on the requested absolute
normalized error.  This is the density of the deterministic forced
subset, with the original $t>2$ threshold preserved.
The canonical declarations are
\texttt{ValuationDensityBounds.effective\_count\_bounds} and
\texttt{ValuationDensityBounds.uniform\_density\_approximation}.

\paragraph{Charge the rank of the forcing event.}
Let $G$ be the complete retained start set.  On the actual prime-bit
space up to $M$, impose the linear equations
$Y_{x-1}=1$ and $Y_{x+j}=0$ for $x\in G$, $0\leq j<q-1$.
The one-negative-prime sample proves that this affine system is
compatible.  If its effective rank is $r$, its exact mass is $2^{-r}$,
and rank--nullity gives $r\leq\pi(M)$.  Both full exponential moments
are therefore at least $\exp\{tC(N,q)-r\log2\}$ for $t\geq0$.
Either earlier lower count budget may replace $C(N,q)$ in this bound.

A prime dividing none of the observed integers supplies a nonzero
homogeneous kernel vector by flipping just its bit, so $r<\pi(M)$.
For $N=18$, $q=3$, the retained multipliers are exactly $7,8,9,10,11$,
giving five starts.  Their observed integers lie between $21$ and $35$.
The supported cutoff $36$ contains eleven primes, but the prime $19$
is unused.  Thus $r\leq10$, and both full moments are at least
$\exp(5t)/1024$, compared with $\exp(5t)/2048$ from the same-count
single-atom bound.  This gives at least a factor-two gain, with no
assertion of a new asymptotic threshold or an exact general rank formula.
The canonical declarations are
\texttt{RankChargedPrimeCylinder.retained\_forcing\_mass},
\texttt{positive\_explicit\_example} and \texttt{total\_explicit\_example}.

\paragraph{Realize the positional target by independent arrivals.}
For the original ground $I_N$ and good-edge family $E$, put
$\iota_x=\sum_{e\in E:x\in e}q_e$.
Take mutually independent variables
$U_x\sim\operatorname{Pois}(p-\iota_x)$ and
$V_e\sim\operatorname{Pois}(q_e)$, with the original $p=2^{-L}$
and individual joint rates $q_e$.
The field $Z_x=U_x+\sum_{e\in E:x\in e}V_e$ has exactly the
completed positional target law.  A finite product of Poisson measures
constructs these arrivals, and every independent realization has the
same original-grid probabilities.  Each reference coordinate has exactly
law $\operatorname{Pois}(p)$.  For every $j\in\mathbb N$, its falling-factorial
test is integrable and $\mathbb E[(Z_x)_j]=p^j$.  The finite marginal
statement also holds for arbitrary finite packets. Each reference coordinate
is square integrable, and the exact shared-arrival formula is
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_x,Z_y)=\boldsymbol{1}_{x=y}(p-\iota_x)
       +\sum_{e\in E:x,y\in e}q_e.
\]
For pair packets the variance is $p$, and the distinct-site covariance is
$q_{\{x,y\}}$ on a retained edge and zero otherwise. These are identities
for the reference arrivals; they do not identify the covariance of the
original arithmetic indicators. Two distinct reference coordinates are
independent if and only if $\sum_{e\in E:x,y\in e}q_e=0$. For pairs,
they are dependent exactly on retained edges with $q_e>0$; zero-rate
edges create no dependence. This criterion covers the natural-valued
counts and their real versions in every independent realization.
For each fixed degree $j$ and $\varepsilon>0$, one threshold precedes
the critical lengths, probability spaces and arrival variables.  The series
\[
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb N^{I_N}}(1+\textstyle\sum_x z_x)^j
 \bigl|\Pr(\text{actual unsigned field}=z)-\Pr(Z=z)\bigr|
\]
is summable and at most $N^{-4/5+\varepsilon}$.
This is the independent-arrival form of main Lemma~20.1, with its
original grid, rates and exponent.

\paragraph{Scope and reproducibility.}
The smooth-window proof above specializes the physical localization to the
ordinary smooth indicator.  A general sharp short-average theorem for
all real completely multiplicative functions, and the full Dickman
relative-density asymptotic, are outside its claimed scope.
The exact integer-source geometric estimates used in
Lemma~\ref{p1:arith:geometric} are also proved internally; the unrestricted
general surface estimate is not required for those source families.
The repository records each correction, strengthened estimate,
quantifier clarification and intermediate improvement in its
machine-readable mathematical change ledger, with the corresponding
Lean declarations and validation commits.
The final audits use only the kernel axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
