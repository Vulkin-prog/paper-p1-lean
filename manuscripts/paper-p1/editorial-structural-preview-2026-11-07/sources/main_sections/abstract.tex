\begin{abstract}
Let \(f\) be a completely multiplicative function whose values
at primes are independent fair signs. We count starts of
constant runs of length at least \(L\) in \([N,2N)\), with
\(L=\log_2N+O(1)\) and both signs included.
The distance from the Poisson law with nominal or exact mean
has an explicit asymptotic equivalent of order \(LN^{-2/3}\),
with a coefficient depending on \(N2^{-L}\) and \(L\bmod3\).
Using the exact mean and the exact second factorial cumulant,
the quadratic Poisson correction has optimal \(\ell^1\) error
power \(N^{-4/3}\): for every
\(\varepsilon>0\), it lies between constant multiples of
\(N^{-4/3-\varepsilon}\) and \(N^{-4/3+\varepsilon}\),
uniformly in each fixed critical band.

We also determine the optimal error for each fixed finite
truncation of the rational pair channels. Keeping all heights
at most \(h\) gives power \(h/(h+1)\) and a leading equivalent
from the first omitted height. The third factorial cumulant
has optimal power \(41/30\), unconditionally.
The proof keeps exact Bernoulli marginals, stops the expansion
before large dependent configurations accumulate, and controls
triples through arithmetic rigidity of pairs.
Short prime-factor witnesses force a common algebraic curve;
a degree-uniform integer-point estimate then limits their
rank. A fourth Charlier test detects the pair-pair contribution
responsible for optimality of the quadratic error exponent.
\end{abstract}
