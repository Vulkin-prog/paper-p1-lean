2. stopped.tex — Replace the existing general attribution paragraph

The first identity is finite and requires no independence. It is a
specialization of Zeilberger's abstract lace expansion
\cite[formula~(*)]{zeilberger1997}. The broader inclusion--exclusion
context includes the abstract-tube identities of Naiman and Wynn and
of Dohmen \cite{naimanwynn1997,dohmen1999}. We give the prefix proof
and its convolution form. The model-specific work is the choice of
the retained arithmetic class and the uniform estimate of its full
boundary. Its exact-marginal application will reduce the approximation
problem to families whose effective nullity is at most $2L$.

4. introduction.tex — After the classical Poisson--Charlier paragraph

\v{C}ekanavi\v{c}ius and Vellaisamy
\cite[Theorems~3.1--3.2, equations~(3.5)--(3.6)]{cekanaviciusvellaisamy2015}
compare quadratic and exponentiated corrections in total variation
for locally dependent sums. Their dependence and moment assumptions
do not cover the separated arithmetic windows considered here.
Asymptotic distance equivalents also arise in the mod-$\phi$ schemes
of Chhaibi, Delbaen, M\'eliot, and Nikeghbali
\cite[Theorem~3.11]{chhaibietal2020}. That theorem uses a parameter
tending to infinity and regularity assumptions on the Fourier
residues; our Poisson means remain in a compact subset of $(0,\infty)$.

5. quartic_lower.tex — Optional brief attribution near the opening

The quartic term generated by the square of the second factorial
cumulant is classical; see
\cite[Section~8.1]{bkn2014} and the comparison of quadratic and
exponentiated corrections in
\cite[Theorems~3.1--3.2]{cekanaviciusvellaisamy2015}.
The work here is to control the remaining arithmetic contributions
and justify the unbounded Charlier test using independently proved
moment bounds.

6. discussion.tex — After the first paragraph, before the positional comparison

The distinction between a quadratic correction and its convolution
exponential is classical. For locally dependent sums,
\v{C}ekanavi\v{c}ius and Vellaisamy
\cite[Theorems~3.1--3.2, equations~(3.5)--(3.6)]{cekanaviciusvellaisamy2015}
give corresponding total-variation bounds, with a quadratic remainder
term present for the first reference and absent for the exponential.
Here the arithmetic dependence is long-range; the estimates concern
the channel contributions and the remainder for the present run count.
